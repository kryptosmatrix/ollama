## 2. Method16 R-3.1/R-3.2 for the 12-line repair

**R-3.1** (`Method/16:169`): *"The blueprint MUST carry an **itemised** production estimate: per pack, per file, with target line counts. A single aggregate number is not an estimate. **An absent estimate is BLOCKING in its own right**."*

**R-3.2** (`Method/16:170`): *"Total document **bytes** — the whole file, tables included — MUST NOT exceed **40 × the itemised estimate in LOC**."*

**Arithmetic.** Draft = 10,541 bytes. Itemised estimate = 12 LOC. 40 × 12 = 480 bytes. 10,541 > 480. The ratio is exceeded by a factor of ~22.

**Is counting all existing target-file lines supported?** No. R-3.1 requires an *itemised production estimate* — lines the blueprint will add or change. `database.go` (1,371 lines) and `store.go` (542 lines) are the *target files*, not the estimate. Counting their total lines would be exactly the inflation R-3.2's "never by raising the estimate alone" clause (`:170`) prohibits. The estimate is 12.

**Is the ratio a real blocker?** Yes, on the face of R-3.2. The draft as written cannot pass R-3.2 at 12 LOC.

**Existing applicable branch.** `Method/16 §2.2` AS-BUILT branch (`:99-104`): *"A blueprint is **as-built** when every behaviour it specifies is already live at `source_sha`, so its production estimate is zero."* The draft is **not** as-built — it specifies behaviour that does not yet exist (the corrected error classification). So the AS-BUILT branch does not apply. The draft is a build-facing document with a 12-line estimate.

**The smallest explicit operator decision needed if rules cannot be satisfied together.** R-3.2's discharge options are: *"lossless restructuring or deletion of redundant prose, or by DECOMPOSE after §6.4a independently proves multiple features; never by raising the estimate alone or splitting one feature to escape the ratio."* The draft is one feature (chat-read integrity). DECOMPOSE is not available. So the only R-3.2-compliant path is lossless restructuring of the draft to ≤480 bytes — which is not feasible for a document carrying six frozen requirements, a proof contract, and a false-implementation table. The operator decision required is therefore: **either (a) authorise a Tier 1 classification** (which R-3.2 does not exempt, but which reduces the rule set), **or (b) accept that the draft must be restructured to a much shorter form**, **or (c) record an explicit operator override** under `Method/16 §7` — *"Operator override of any exit | Recorded in the header as an override naming what was not earned. An override MUST NOT be recorded as `[IMPLEMENTATION-READY]`."* The override is the only mechanism that does not smuggle a waiver.

**Distinguishing blueprint-promotion rules from maintenance rules.** R-3.1/R-3.2 are blueprint-promotion rules. They govern the *document*, not the *repair*. `Method/04` governs the repair itself and imposes no byte ratio. A 12-line repair is not exempt from R-3.1/R-3.2 because it is small; it is subject to them because it is specified in a blueprint. The correct reading is: the blueprint must satisfy R-3.1/R-3.2 to earn its rung; the repair then proceeds under `Method/04` at that rung.
